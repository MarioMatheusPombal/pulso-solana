# PULSO · design system and brand kit v2

This guide defines the PULSO design system and expressive brand kit. The Guardian identity was selected and is applied to the web app, the READMEs, the public docs and the demo video.

**NOT AUDITED · DEVNET DEMONSTRATION ONLY**

## 1. Product and position

PULSO is the human authorization layer an AI agent has to pass through. The agent may hold the wallet; the authority stays with the person who grants and limits the permissions.

> Give agents money without giving them unlimited power.

> The agent holds the wallet. The human holds the authority.

Explain the product as the enforcement of authority granted by humans. PULSO is not a shopping agent, procurement software, a recommender or an intent detector.

The name "Pulso" (pulse) evokes presence, continuity and a signal that deserves attention. Use that idea as a visual and verbal tone. Do not turn the metaphor into a promise of medical monitoring, guaranteed protection, certification or absolute security.

## 2. Current identity

The visual direction is the expressive Guardian: amber eyes, light mask, dark hood and long beak. The expression of the chosen PNG is the main element. Variations keep the art and change the background or composition. The earlier geometric exploration is archived in the gallery.

## 3. Personality and language

Speak with calm, precision and respect for the human decision. Show what is being authorized and what consequence follows.

- Prefer "review", "authorize", "deny", "expires" and "confirmed on devnet".
- Avoid "the agent understands your intent", "fully secure", "protected against everything" and "audited".
- Use short sentences in decision states. Name the action and the observed result.
- Do not use plague humor, horror, skulls, gore, syringes, medical crosses or seals that look like certification.
- Do not use the guardian's name or metaphor to suggest clinical care or a security guarantee.

## 4. Brand family

### 4.1 Preferred art · expressive Guardian

The art in `raster-v2/` follows the preferred face and expression. Use [guardian-mark-transparent.png](raster-v2/guardian-mark-transparent.png) as the compact mark and [guardian-heart-transparent.png](raster-v2/guardian-heart-transparent.png) as the campaign mascot. Both are RGBA PNGs of 1254 × 1254 px, generated by editing `guardian-contrast-study.png` as a reference. The edit preserves the chosen visual language, not the original pixels. See [IMAGEGEN_PROMPTS.md](IMAGEGEN_PROMPTS.md) for provenance and prompts.

Use the full illustration in campaign compositions and the compact head in logos, lockups, avatars and favicons. Transparency allows the same art to be composed over light, dark and amber backgrounds.

### 4.2 Compact mark, wordmark and lockups

Horizontal and stacked lockups combine the original PNG image with the editable wordmark. An SVG in the kit is a composition container; the embedded art remains raster.

Use the light/dark files according to the background. Keep proportional scale and clearance around the symbol and wordmark. The earlier geometric family is archived as a study.

### 4.3 Avatar and favicon

Use the avatar and favicon variants in the kit, choosing the background that preserves expression and contrast. Avatars keep a margin for circular cropping. Favicons are size-specific files.

Do not use the full illustration as a favicon. Small symbols cannot carry the public notice legibly; keep the notice in the page or composition context that accompanies them.

### 4.4 Protection area

Keep a minimum margin of one quarter of the compact mark's height on each side. Keep more space next to text, edges or controls.

Do not distort the art or add effects. Use the background color or variants produced by the kit to preserve contrast. Gallery tiles are not part of the brand.

## 5. Color

The values in `tokens.css` and `tokens.json` define the colors of the v2 kit. `#C0C4C8` is the gallery-local value for secondary text on the dark theme.

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas / ivory | `#F6F4EF` | Light background and reading area |
| Ink / charcoal | `#14171A` | Main text and mark |
| Amber | `#FFB020` | Attention and a request awaiting a decision |
| Panel / white | `#FFFFFF` | Raised light surface |
| Muted | `#62686D` | Secondary text on the light theme |
| Dark canvas | `#0A0E13` | Dark theme background |
| Dark panel | `#10161D` | Dark theme surface |
| Dark muted, gallery | `#C0C4C8` | Secondary text on the dark theme |
| Success | `#14734B` | Confirmed result |
| Danger | `#B42318` | Denied action or observed failure |
| Focus | `#2563EB` | Keyboard focus indicator |
| Dark success | `#55D38F` | Confirmation on a dark surface |
| Dark danger | `#FF7D72` | Denial or failure on a dark surface |
| Dark focus | `#8ABAFF` | Keyboard focus on a dark surface |
| Coral | `#C6534C` | Optional editorial accent, outside the states |

Do not use amber as a synonym for "approved". Green means a confirmed result and only appears after that result is observed. Red means denial or failure. Expiry uses a label and neutral treatment. Blue identifies focus, not transaction state.

Always combine color with text and, when useful, an icon or shape. Do not convey states by color alone.

### 5.1 Reference contrast

Ratios computed with the WCAG relative luminance formula. Values are rounded; recompute if any token changes. Normal text requires 4.5:1; large text and essential graphical elements require 3:1.

| Text / element on background | Ratio | Guidance |
|---|---:|---|
| Charcoal on ivory | 16.37:1 | Normal text |
| Light muted on ivory | 5.14:1 | Secondary text |
| Charcoal on amber | 9.84:1 | Button and label text |
| White on green | 5.86:1 | Confirmation text |
| White on red | 6.57:1 | Denial or failure text |
| White on blue | 5.17:1 | Text on blue, if used |
| Ivory on dark charcoal | 17.61:1 | Main text on the dark theme |
| Dark muted `#C0C4C8` on dark canvas | 11.03:1 | Dark secondary text |
| Dark muted on dark panel | 10.37:1 | Secondary text in a dark panel |
| Dark success on dark panel | 9.61:1 | Dark confirmed state |
| Dark danger on dark panel | 7.30:1 | Dark error state |
| Dark focus on dark panel | 9.14:1 | Dark focus indicator |
| Amber on ivory | 1.66:1 | Graphic accent; do not use as small text |

The dark theme needs the specific light tokens for secondary text, confirmation, error and focus. Do not reuse the light `#62686D`, green, red or blue on a dark background without recomputing contrast. Coral `#C6534C` is an editorial accent; raster studies may show other coral tones, which do not replace the token.

## 6. Typography

In the gallery and in the product, use the system sans-serif for titles, body and controls, and a monospace for technical data. The SVG compositions in the kit use Arial/Helvetica for the wordmark and labels; those fonts are not vendored.

```css
font-family: system-ui, -apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", sans-serif;
font-family: ui-monospace, SFMono-Regular, Consolas, monospace;
```

IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono are optional alternatives, not files of this kit. See the [IBM typography documentation](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/typography/typeface/) and the [SIL Open Font License 1.1](https://github.com/IBM/plex/blob/master/LICENSE.txt) before including them in another distribution.

| Level | Size | Line height | Use |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Display | 48 px | 1.0–1.1 | Wide headline, only in a layout with room |
| Title 1 | 40 px | 1.1 | Adaptable main heading |
| Title 2 | 32 px | 1.2 | Section |
| Title 3 | 24 px | 1.25 | Group or card |
| Subtitle | 20 px | 1.35 | Short introduction |
| Body | 16 px | 1.5 | Running text and review |
| Label | 14 px | 1.4 | Field, button and state |
| Auxiliary | 12 px | 1.4 | Secondary context; do not use for critical data |

Use weights 700–800 in headlines, 600–700 in actions and 400–500 in text. Avoid tracking in addresses, hashes and values. Allow line wrapping or copying of the whole value; never cut information needed to review an authorization.

## 7. Spacing and layout

Use the pixel scale in `tokens.css`: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64. Use smaller steps inside controls and larger ones between regions.

| Spacing token | Value | Example |
|---|---:|---|
| `space-1` | 4 px | Icon/label separation |
| `space-2` | 8 px | Compact content |
| `space-3` | 12 px | Field and short grouping |
| `space-4` | 16 px | Control and small card padding |
| `space-6` | 24 px | Panel padding |
| `space-8` | 32 px | Separation between blocks |
| `space-12` | 48 px | Section breathing room |
| `space-16` | 64 px | Wide section separation |

Proposed radii: 8 px on controls, 14 px on cards, 22 px on highlight panels and a pill only on state labels. Use neutral borders; shadow stays subtle and does not replace separation or focus.

Start from one column on narrow screens. At 320 px, keep content and action visible without horizontal scrolling. On wide screens, limit reading to about 70 characters per line. For dense layouts, prefer two columns that collapse to one at 800 px, as in the current gallery.

## 8. Icons

Functional icons must be simple, consistent and subordinate to the label. Use a 24 px grid, a 2 px stroke, rounded terminals and an interactive area of at least 44 × 44 px. At 16 px, reduce detail instead of thinning the stroke.

Do not use the guardian to represent every command or state. Do not use shield, cross, padlock or check pictograms as a security claim. Critical states need explicit words.

## 9. Interface components

These rules define how to apply the identity to the PULSO interface. The gallery shows a static composition, not production components.

### 9.1 Buttons

- Primary: amber `#FFB020` with charcoal text. The text names the action, such as "Approve once".
- Secondary: neutral background, visible border and main text; use for "View details" or navigation.
- Denial: a neutral button with the text "Deny". Reserve red for the recorded consequence, not for pressuring a decision.
- Hover: change the surface or border discreetly; preserve label and contrast.
- Pressed: indicate the interaction without shifting or hiding the text.
- Focus: a 3 px blue outline with a 3 px offset, visible on keyboard and in high contrast.
- Disabled: remove the keyboard action and show a nearby explanation. Opacity alone does not explain the reason.
- Loading: keep the action name and announce the state. Prevent duplicate submission; do not simulate confirmation.

### 9.2 Fields and errors

Label every field persistently. Preserve the typed value on a recoverable error. Associate the message with the field and explain how to fix it.

States: default, focus, filled, invalid, disabled and loading. An error uses text, an optional icon and color; do not rely only on a red border. Do not move the field when the message appears.

### 9.3 Cards and panels

Use a panel to group related context, without letting the card appearance become a trust confirmation. Title first, metadata after, action in the expected place. Separate a request awaiting review from completed history.

### 9.4 Authorization request and payload

Present the payload that will be signed exactly and completely. Do not replace the signed data with a parallel summary. Keep a legible label next to the full machine value.

When these fields are part of the real transaction, show mint or asset, recipient, amount in base units, nonce, expiry, use count and program identifier. Do not invent a schema. Follow the implemented schema and the repository's canonical specifications.

Addresses, hashes, limits, nonces and timestamps may use monospace. Do not truncate, hide, round or change the precision of data the person needs to compare. Allow copying the full value. Never expose a private key or secret.

Group the payload panel with one clear action to approve and another to deny. Show the expiry and the number of uses when defined by the authorization. The gallery's static demonstration is illustrative, incomplete and not signable; it does not define a schema.

### 9.5 Tables

Use explicit headers and column labels. In responsive tables, preserve the relation between value and label when rearranging rows. Numbers align right; long identifiers may wrap without losing characters.

Do not use row color as the only indication of selection, failure or authorization. Provide state text and visible focus on interactive controls.

### 9.6 Badges and states

| Visual state | Color | Required text | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awaiting person | Amber | "Awaiting approval" | Human decision needed |
| Confirmed | Green | "Confirmed" or the observed result | Only after the response observed on-chain |
| Denied/failed | Red | "Denied" or a description of the failure | Result actually known |
| Expired | Neutral | "Expired" | Authorization window closed |
| Focus | Blue | Existing label stays | Control receives keyboard focus |

Icon and shape may reinforce the state; color never communicates it alone. Do not show "confirmed" in advance or present a visual badge as proof of security.

## 10. Accessibility and interaction

- Keep keyboard focus visible on links, buttons and fields.
- Use semantic structure, accessible names and error text associated with the field.
- Do not remove focus to reproduce the gallery's static appearance.
- Use an adequate touch area and a focus order that follows reading.
- Allow zoom and text enlargement without losing an action.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Test contrast in every real combination of text, background, border and state.
- Offer state in text in addition to color, animation, sound or shape.

## 11. Motion and video

Use 180–240 ms transitions only to indicate a surface or focus change. Do not animate, change, shuffle or scroll the payload values while the person reviews.

Reduce or remove motion for anyone who requests reduced motion. All information and action must remain available without animation.

### 20-second storyboard

| Time | Scene |
|---|---|
| 0–3 s | Agent requests an action |
| 3–8 s | Exact request fields appear and stay stable |
| 8–13 s | Person reviews the payload |
| 13–16 s | Single-use approval is triggered |
| 16–20 s | Result observed on devnet and demonstration notice |

The layers for animation, the timing previews, the motion tokens and the README assets are in [`motion/`](motion/MOTION.md) and `../readme/`.

The kit includes an [opening still](expressive-kit/video/opening-head.png) and a [closing still](expressive-kit/video/closing-heart.png), both 1920 × 1080 px, with SVG equivalents for editing text and layout. They are brand frames; they include no MP4 or recording of the demonstration. Keep the payload values stable while the person reviews.

## 12. Compositions and channels

The brand kit includes editable SVG compositions and PNGs for horizontal, square and story campaigns, on light and dark backgrounds. The SVG keeps text and layout editable and embeds the original PNG art; the PNG is the export ready to share.

Campaign videos, presentations and public images must include, legibly and in full:

> NOT AUDITED · DEVNET DEMONSTRATION ONLY

A favicon or tiny symbol cannot hold a legible notice. Include it in the page, presentation or composition that presents the symbol.

`inventory.json` records 84 exports: 43 PNGs, 40 SVGs and one ICO, with names, dimensions, bytes and hashes. `build.js` recreates `expressive-kit/` from the base PNGs, without changing the sources in `raster-v2/`. Run it at the root of the source repository (where this kit lives under `public/assets/brand-v1/`):

```sh
node public/assets/brand-v1/expressive-kit/build.js
```

The build requires Sharp available in Node. If Sharp is not in the default Node module path, set `SHARP_MODULE` to the module to load. The kit uses system sans in the UI and Arial/Helvetica in compositions; it does not include IBM Plex fonts.

## 14. Public use and limits

All public demonstration material must show **NOT AUDITED · DEVNET DEMONSTRATION ONLY**. Do not claim audit, medical efficacy, production security or absolute protection.

## 15. Application to the product

Use these rules when applying the kit in the app: preserve the expressive PNG in lockups; keep background variants coherent; use the state colors with label and icon; keep focus visible; and respect reduced motion. On the approval screen, show exactly the payload that will be signed, complete and copyable. Do not replace the signed values with a parallel summary, do not invent a schema and do not animate those values during review.
